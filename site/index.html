In honour of

Seventy years since Ghana became free.

On 6 March 1957, Ghana declared its independence. On 6 March 2027 it reaches its Platinum Jubilee, and London gathers to honour it.

  1. 6 March 1957The nationAt midnight Ghana became free and independent, the Black Star that lit the way for a continent.
  2. Seventy yearsThe peopleThe generations who built Ghana, and the Ghanaians whose lives have enriched Britain for centuries.
  3. TodayThe friendshipBetween Ghana and the United Kingdom, and everyone, of every heritage, who loves Ghana.

An independent celebration, organised by Ghana at 70 UK and open to everyone.

Partner spotlight

The partners behind the celebration.

  • Creative Partner

    TwinsDntBeg, leading the Creative Hub

    Twin brothers Emmanuel and Samuel Appiah Gyan are the Ghanaian photography and film duo behind Swag of Africa Studios. In November 2026 they announced a collaboration with Canon to develop the next generation of African photographers, filmmakers and creators. Their work with Rocky Dawuni on Rise was nominated for Music Film of the Year at the 2025 Black Stars International Film Festival. They curate Seventy Years in Frame: seventy photographs, a film of seven decades and the Creative Showcase on Independence Day.

    Seventy Years in FrameVisit TwinsDntBeg
  • Fashion Partner Ghana

    Quophi Akotuah, leading the Fashion Show

    A luxury fashion house founded in Accra in 2015 by Michael Addo, known as Quophi Michaels, and crowned King of the Glitz Style Awards in 2017. Quophi Akotuah leads the team of Ghanaian fashion houses joining us in London for the Ghana at 70 Fashion Show, open to every house, and offers guests a ticket holder price on made to measure outfits, with a final fitting in London.

    The Fashion ShowVisit Quophi Akotuah

    Impact Africa, leading the Business Hub

    Impact Africa is a UK to Africa business, investment and market access platform, founded by Kwabena Anoff. Through its business accelerator, Africa investment roadshows and the annual Impact Africa Business and Investment Summit, it connects founders with capital, markets and partners. Seventy years · One family

The Motherland is us.

Somewhere in Barking tonight, a mother is ironing a Kente cloth she has kept for thirty years.

She wore it the day she landed. She wore it to every wedding, every outdooring and every graduation. On the fifth of March she will wear it again.

Continue the story

In Manchester, a young man is choosing a Black Stars shirt. In Birmingham, two aunties are already arguing about whose jollof is best. In Glasgow, a student is booking the train south. In Dublin, a family is booking the flight over. In Amsterdam and Hamburg, cousins are comparing flights. In New York, Houston and Toronto, an uncle has already booked. Some of us were born in Osu, some in Kumasi, some in Leeds and others in south London, and every one of us knows the words of that midnight in 1957: Ghana, your beloved country, is free forever.

On 6 March 1957 our grandparents danced until morning. Now it is our turn. From every corner of the UK, from Europe, from North America and from Ghana itself, Ghanaians are coming together in one great hall for three days: The Royal Jubilee Gala on Independence Eve, the Ghana Day Festival on Independence Day itself, and Women of the Motherland on Mothering Sunday.

Red for those who gave their lives for freedom. Gold for the wealth of the land. Green for its forests and fields. And the Black Star, the lodestar of African freedom. Seventy years on, we honour every colour of the flag.

Ghana is seventy. Ghana is ours. Come and celebrate Ghana.
