The style guide

The art of wearing Kente.

Read it like a map. Every detail has a meaning.

How to wear Kente

For kings

  1. 1
    The full cloth

    One full cloth, woven strip by strip and sewn together. Every pattern has a name and a meaning.

  2. 2
    Right shoulder bare

    The cloth passes under the right arm, so the right shoulder stays bare, the way the chiefs wear it.

  3. 3
    Over the left shoulder

    The long end is thrown over the left shoulder and falls down the back.

  4. 4
    Ahenema sandals

    Royal leather sandals, handmade. With black tie, polished shoes.

For queens

  1. 5
    The headpiece

    A gold band or a sculpted duku, tall and proud for the Gala.

  2. 6
    Beads and gold

    Krobo beads at the neck, gold at the wrist and the arm. Wear the pieces handed down to you.

  3. 7
    Two cloths

    A wrapped cloth to the ankle and an upper cloth across the body, or a kaba and slit in Kente.

  4. 8
    Jewelled sandals

    Ahenema or jewelled evening sandals, never trainers.

How to wrap the cloth, in four moves.

  1. 1Open the cloth

    Hold the cloth open behind you, the top edge level with your shoulders.

  2. 2Under the right arm

    Bring the right end under your right arm and across the chest. The right shoulder stays bare.

  3. 3Over the left shoulder

    Throw the long end over your left shoulder so it falls down your back.

  4. 4Gather and hold

    Gather the folds over your left arm and hold them in your left hand. Walk slowly, with grace.

The etiquette of the evening

Small courtesies. A memorable night.

A few quiet customs that make the Gala feel like the occasion it is.

  1. Arrive on timeDoors at 18:30 for 20:00. The last guests are received at 19:45, so that dinner is served to every table together.
  2. Wait for the toastAt midnight the hall rises together for the toast to Ghana. Please keep phones away for that moment, and for the reading at 11:00 on Saturday.
  3. Honour the eldersTraditional leaders are greeted as Nana, and elders are greeted first.
  4. Kente is worn with respectFor men, the cloth is draped over the left shoulder. Choose the colours you love. Every pattern carries a meaning.
  5. DecorationsMiniature decorations and orders may be worn with Black Tie, ball gown or Kente.
  6. Leave gracefullyCarriages at 00:30. Our hosts will point every guest to a taxi or to the Night Tube at Bank.
Dress code questions

Before you dress.

Still unsure? Call Asantewaa on +44 7824 555307 and she will help.

Do I have to wear Kente to the Gala?

No. This is a gala dinner and ball. A ball gown, a long evening gown, a dinner jacket or a fugu smock are all perfect. Kente is welcome, never expected.

Can I wear a short dress to the Gala?

The Gala is a long gown evening. Please choose a floor length or ankle length gown, or Kente cloth, rather than a short dress. Black is always welcome.

I am not Ghanaian. Can I wear Kente?

Yes. Kente and Ghanaian cloth are for everyone, worn with respect and joy. New to Kente? Add it when you book and we help you wear it properly.

Can I wear the dress of another African nation?

Yes. Wear your heritage proudly. Agbada, boubou, kitenge and every royal dress of the continent belong at the Gala.

Where can I find Kente in time?

Add Kente and gold when you book and our Kente partners will hold sashes, stoles and accessories for you, ready to collect.

I am in mourning. Can I still come?

Yes, and you are very welcome. Come as you are. Black is always welcome, and so is your mourning cloth. This is a family celebration, and family includes everyone.

What do I wear on my head?

A headwrap or gele is the crown of this event. Keep it clear of the view of the guests behind you.

Is there a cloakroom?

Yes, staffed from the moment doors open on every day.

Will I be refused entry?

We would rather welcome you than turn you away. Please follow the dress code: guests in sportswear at the Gala may be asked to change.
